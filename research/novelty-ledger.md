# Research novelty ledger

This ledger records proposal status; it is not a claim of novelty. A 17-topic first prior-art batch is documented in [`prior-art-index.md`](prior-art-index.md) and [`prior-art-synthesis.md`](prior-art-synthesis.md). The broader review is incomplete, and QML has not independently reproduced any of the surveyed methods or results.

| Proposal | Current description | Evidence from first review batch | Status and required next comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relational Morph Quantization (RMQ) | Explore representing layer weights through shared latent structures, layer-specific transforms, and residual information, including the proposed form `W_l ≈ A_l × S × B_l + R_l`. | Related established prior art includes CALDERA’s quantized full-shape backbone plus low-rank factors, tensor-network/MPO methods, Kronecker factorization, learned/additive codebooks, and residual/error-feedback quantization. These are relevant comparison points, not proof of identity with the working RMQ sketch. | No novelty conclusion. Specify the exact shared structures, optimization objective, residual coding, and direct-execution equations; then compare feature by feature against each relevant prior method and implementation. Count complete metadata, factors, codebooks, residuals, and indices. |
| Morphological Adaptive Precision (MAP) | Explore precision allocation based on measured functional importance, potentially including cross-layer error propagation. | Clear prior art includes HAWQ/HAWQ-V2 curvature-based allocation, HAQ hardware-feedback search, AWQ activation saliency, SmoothQuant reparameterization, and mixed-precision baselines. Broadly allocating precision by sensitivity is therefore not itself a novelty claim. The acronym “MAP” is also used in prior work for maximum a posteriori code assignment, including AQLM-related discussion; QML’s full name should be written to avoid ambiguity. | No novelty conclusion. Define the new allocation rule and objective precisely; compare against established sensitivity/activation-aware methods on matched models, tasks, calibration, and resource budgets, including cross-layer propagation ablations. |
| Quantum Morph Format (QMF) | Proposed portable, versioned container for multiple representations and execution metadata. | The first review covers GGUF/GGML and related storage/representation concepts, but does not fully survey model containers. The QML directive’s QMF description is incomplete and the binary specification is not implemented. | No novelty conclusion. Obtain the complete directive, define the binary format and invariants, then compare against existing formats and assess parser safety, reproducibility, compatibility, license metadata, and execution requirements. |
| Quantum-inspired cognitive research | Speculative classical-computation projects involving bounded latent states, signed/complex representations, correlations, and adaptive computation. | Not reviewed in the first batch. No quantum-computing or quantum-advantage claim is supported. | Untested and speculative. Define classical mathematics, complexity bounds, comparison baselines, and falsifiable success criteria before implementation; separately review prior art on quantum-inspired optimization, variational quantum algorithms, complex-valued networks, and information theory. |

## Change protocol

For every proposal, add dated source-backed comparisons, rejected formulations, experiment identifiers, and evidence labels. Keep established literature, QML hypotheses, independently reproduced results, and QML contributions distinct. Do not use “novel,” “world-first,” “patentable,” or performance-superiority language without compelling evidence and review. Preserve failed hypotheses.

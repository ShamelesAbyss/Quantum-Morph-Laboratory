# Quantum Morph Laboratory — prior-art index

This index consolidates the completed **first batch** of prior-art research recorded in the linked topic notes. It is a navigational summary, not a substitute for the notes: reported measurements remain attributed to their papers, maintainers, or project documentation, and none is presented as independently reproduced by QML. The table covers this first batch only; it **does not establish novelty for RMQ or MAP and is not a complete prior-art review**. Where the QML concepts are not sufficiently specified in the records, overlap is explicitly left unknown rather than inferred.

## Synthesis

The batch establishes several **different kinds of baseline**, not one interchangeable compression method. GGUF/GGML provides a concrete storage and conventional block-quantization reference; it is important to separate a file container from the quantizer encoded in its tensor types. GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, HQQ, QuIP/QuIP#, and related calibration-driven methods instead modify or approximate existing model weights after training, but optimize different proxies: layer-output reconstruction with second-order compensation, activation saliency/channel scaling, activation-to-weight rescaling, weight reconstruction, or Hessian-weighted rounding and codebooks. AQLM extends this landscape with learned additive codebooks. Their reported metrics, bitwidths, calibration sets, and system setups differ, so the records do not support a single cross-paper ranking.

A second baseline family changes the **parameterization or structure**: TT/MPS, TR, Tucker, Kronecker factorization, and MPO compression. These methods trade dense parameters for factors/cores, with ranks, mode ordering, or bond dimensions controlling capacity. CALDERA explicitly combines a quantized full-size backbone with low-rank factors, which makes it a particularly relevant comparator to a low-rank-plus-residual proposal, though its stated formulation is not identical to the RMQ sketch. CompactifAI adds layer sensitivity profiling and healing after MPO truncation. Published results vary by architecture and task, and parameter reduction alone does not imply faster inference; several records explicitly show that kernel implementation, factor application, and device behavior govern realized speed.

A third set addresses **precision allocation and discrete representations**. HAWQ/HAWQ-V2 use curvature-derived sensitivity proxies, while HAQ adds hardware feedback to mixed-precision search. Those are clear prior-art comparators for any MAP proposal that assigns precision by importance or budget. Vector/product quantization and AQLM establish codebook-based representations; VQ-VAE and learned image-compression work establish learned discrete latents and rate-distortion/entropy coding. These are useful methodological precedents, but their objectives and application targets differ: image rate-distortion does not establish preservation of a model’s downstream function, and vector-search recall does not establish neural-task quality. BitNet is a distinct train-from-scratch route rather than a generic post-training conversion; its theoretical alphabet size should not be mistaken for physical bits per stored weight or universal inference speed.

Recurring trade-offs across this batch are (1) compression versus reconstruction or task quality; (2) lower nominal precision/parameter count versus metadata, codebook, factor, and residual overhead; (3) more elaborate calibration, optimization, or fine-tuning versus encoding cost and implementation complexity; and (4) theoretical FLOP, memory, or energy advantages versus realized performance on supported kernels and hardware. Calibration distribution and model/task conditions matter throughout. “Effective bits,” average bits excluding floating-point parameters, model-file size, and runtime memory are not interchangeable measures. Similarly, paper-reported timings, author-maintainer tables, and independently reproduced results are different evidence classes. **No QML method or cited numerical result in this batch has been independently reproduced by QML.**

Important unreviewed scope remains. RMQ, MAP, and especially QMF lack complete formal specifications in the supplied records, so precise identity, derivation, novelty, and format-level comparisons remain open. The batch is not exhaustive of quantization, mixed precision, pruning, distillation, low-rank/tensor networks, entropy coding, model formats, or patent literature; nor does it provide a systematic search protocol or legal assessment. Licensing is artifact-specific: several software repositories are permissively licensed, but code licenses do not grant model-weight rights. Explicit gaps include the unverified Hugging Face GGUF parser-package license, QuIP’s original code license, CALDERA’s operative software license, CompactifAI’s core implementation/derived weights, and various checkpoint/model terms. These gaps and the per-note caveats should be resolved before reuse, and broader review is needed before any novelty or freedom-to-operate conclusion.
